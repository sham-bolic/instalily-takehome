# DuPont Tedlar Aerospace ICP notes

Retrieved: 2026-08-18

## First-party findings

DuPont positions Tedlar PVF film as surface protection for aircraft interiors. It highlights design flexibility, configurable textures, gloss levels, thicknesses and finishes, fade-resistant colors, clear overlaminates for printed patterns, cleanability, and scuff resistance.[1]

DuPont identifies these aircraft applications:

- Interior ceiling and sidewall decorative panels
- Window shades
- Stow bins
- Lavatories and galleys
- Personal service units
- Bulkhead partitions
- Insulation and moisture barriers
- Cargo bin liners
- Aircraft wire markers
- Composite noise panels

DuPont also states that Tedlar film can be thermoformed and that its quality management systems are certified to AS9100:2016 and ISO 9001:2015.[1] Its broader aerospace page positions Tedlar around easy-to-clean, scuff-resistant aircraft interior surfaces.[2]

Safran describes Safran Cabin as a world leader in aircraft interiors and systems. That product scope and commercial scale make it a useful representative ideal company for this ICP, although this does not assert that Safran is a current DuPont customer.[3]

## Ideal Customer Profile

### Offering

DuPont Tedlar PVF film for aircraft interiors: a configurable, thermoformable protective surface film available in colors, textures, gloss levels, finishes, and clear grades for printed patterns. It helps cabin components retain an attractive, easy-to-clean, scuff-resistant surface over a long service life.

### Ideal customers

Primary: established aircraft cabin-interior component manufacturers and aerospace laminators or converters that select and integrate decorative surface films into thermoplastic, composite, or honeycomb panels. Secondary: aircraft OEM cabin teams, airline engineering groups, and cabin refurbishment or MRO providers that control interior material specifications and recurring retrofit programs.

### Target applications

Aircraft sidewalls and ceilings, decorative panels, window shades, stow bins, lavatories, galleys, personal service units, bulkheads, insulation and moisture barriers, cargo liners, wire markers, and composite noise panels.

### Strong-fit signals

Must-have: manufactures, laminates, thermoforms, refurbishes, or specifies aircraft interior components and can qualify a surface film into an aerospace program. Strong signals: AS9100 or comparable aerospace quality systems; OEM or airline approvals; recurring line-fit or retrofit programs; composite or honeycomb panel production; cabin surfaces exposed to frequent cleaning, scuffing, stains, moisture, or fading; demand for custom colors, textures, printed patterns, or lower-impact cabin materials; and participation in Aircraft Interiors Expo, APEX Global EXPO, MRO Americas, or related cabin-interiors associations.

### Company size

Prioritize established Tier 1 and Tier 2 cabin suppliers with multiple aircraft programs, OEM or airline relationships, qualified production capacity, and meaningful recurring volume. Smaller specialist laminators and refurbishment firms qualify when they control material specifications and serve repeat fleet programs.

### Geography

Global, prioritizing North America and Europe while including aerospace manufacturing and cabin-refurbishment hubs in Asia and the Middle East.

### Exclusions

Companies focused only on engines, avionics, exterior structures, or seating systems with no relevant interior surface application; general MRO providers that do not replace or specify cabin surfaces; distributors without engineering or specification influence; one-off custom shops without repeat fleet volume; and organizations unable to qualify or integrate film into an aerospace component.

### Representative ideal company

**Safran Cabin**

Safran Cabin operates at global scale in aircraft interiors and systems and supplies the types of cabin structures where durable decorative surface films can be specified. Its engineering role, aerospace qualification capabilities, and recurring aircraft-program exposure make it representative of the target account rather than evidence of an existing DuPont relationship.

## ICP interpretation

The customer categories, fit signals, size preferences, event signals, exclusions, and Safran qualification are sales-targeting interpretations based on the cited product and company information. They are not direct statements by DuPont about its customer list.
